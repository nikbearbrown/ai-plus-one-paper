# Chapter specifications

**Private Blueprint only. Draft route: `ready-for-draft-review`. Evidence: `matched-source`. No sign-off.**

Each entry is a proposed chapter, not drafted prose.

## 1. Represent the Problem Before Choosing a Method

- **Observable capability:** Specify a model and identify its simplifying assumptions.
- **Teaching blocks:** states and goals; actions; objective; model limits
- **Original worked example to write:** A library cart must visit shelves subject to access rules.
- **Assessable practice:** Write a representation and a counterexample to one assumption.

## 2. Search, Cost and Evidence

- **Observable capability:** Predict how a search choice changes work and results.
- **Teaching blocks:** frontiers; objective cost; baseline; trace
- **Original worked example to write:** Two routes serve equal stops but consume different energy.
- **Assessable practice:** Hand-trace a search and compare expansions on a supplied map.

## 3. Reasoning with Incomplete Observations

- **Observable capability:** Compute and interpret a simple uncertain decision.
- **Teaching blocks:** probability model; conditionals; outcomes; sensitivity
- **Original worked example to write:** A greenhouse controller infers a sensor fault from two readings.
- **Assessable practice:** Compute a decision under two assumptions and explain the reversal.

## 4. Learn from Examples and Feedback

- **Observable capability:** Differentiate reasoning given a model, learning given examples, and learning from sequential rewards.
- **Teaching blocks:** supervised targets; train/test split; delayed reward; model comparison
- **Original worked example to write:** A cart designer compares labeled obstacle prediction with a delivery policy.
- **Assessable practice:** Classify three scenarios and justify a different evaluation design for each.

## 5. Evaluate a Decision System

- **Observable capability:** Design a small test that can expose a poor fit.
- **Teaching blocks:** held-out cases; baseline; error types; limitations
- **Original worked example to write:** A scheduling heuristic excels on quiet days but struggles at peak demand.
- **Assessable practice:** Build a benchmark and justify when not to deploy.
