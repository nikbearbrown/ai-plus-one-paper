# Syllabus consolidation decisions

- **Algorithms (07), existing book #4:** one Blueprint combines INFO 6205 across Fall/Spring with the CS3000 proof-oriented course. Keep a proof-oriented track and implementation track within one core algorithmic narrative. Discrete Structures is prerequisite coverage and is not merged merely because both use graphs and proofs.
- **Object-Oriented Design and Design Patterns (19+21):** use one Java-friendly book with a core on contracts, composition, and tested behavior and a second part on pattern selection. Spring CSYE 6200 includes much of the named design-pattern material found in CSYE 6300; earlier sections retain an introductory path.
- **Number Theory (39+40):** one book with foundation and advanced parts. The archived courses continue the same chapter numbering and develop modular arithmetic, ring factorization and later related topics at increasing depth. The explicitly skipped elliptic-curve material in the continuation is not a syllabus requirement; the graduate elliptic-curves/modular-forms course remains separate.
- **Data Engineering for AI (02):** DAMG and INFO labels share one comparison packet. Verify actual scope before treating them as equivalent; do not duplicate based on course codes. General Data Science Engineering and the advanced agent context course have separate outcome tests.
- **Existing exact matches:** the eight directly mapped families plus Design of Agentic Systems and Design Engineering are assimilation packets, not instructions to create competing books.
- **Mobile App Development with Swift (31):** proposed reader-facing subject reflects the available Swift/iOS syllabus rather than the ambiguous schedule label. Hold until the conflict is settled for the intended scope.
- **Data and Context Engineering for Agentic AI (70):** uses the explicit Spring syllabus subtitle; compare the earlier INFO 7390 course materials and row 72 (Data Science Engineering) rather than assuming one program code means one book scope.
- **Near overlaps:** cloud vs. DevOps vs. infrastructure automation, foundational vs. enterprise agent systems, UX research vs. browser development vs. design-to-code, software testing vs. quality management, ML foundations vs. hardware/performance. These remain separate comparison packets unless the writer demonstrates equivalent learner, depth, outcomes, and assessed artifact.
- **No course identity:** Fall Newton CRN 21968 lacks a reliable course identity; the prior section index preserves that unresolved row. No invented title or Blueprint was created for it.
