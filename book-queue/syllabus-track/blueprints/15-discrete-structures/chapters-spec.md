# Chapter specifications

**Private Blueprint only. Draft route: `ready-for-draft-review`. Evidence: `matched-source`. No sign-off.**

Each entry is a proposed chapter, not drafted prose.

## 1. Translate conditions precisely

- **Observable capability:** Use quantifiers and connectives to remove ambiguity.
- **Teaching blocks:** predicates; scope; negation; counterexamples
- **Original worked example to write:** Define availability for a fictional shared studio room.
- **Assessable practice:** Rewrite a policy two ways and find a schedule on which they differ.

## 2. Build a proof and test its claim

- **Observable capability:** Choose direct reasoning or contradiction and identify failed assumptions.
- **Teaching blocks:** sets and implications; direct proof; contradiction; induction and base cases
- **Original worked example to write:** Prove that two membership rules on toy groups are or are not equivalent.
- **Assessable practice:** Submit a written proof and a minimal counterexample to its invalid converse.

## 3. Count without losing cases

- **Observable capability:** Use partition, product or inclusion-exclusion with explicit assumptions.
- **Teaching blocks:** bijection; combinations; overlap; complement
- **Original worked example to write:** Count workshop seat assignments with two restricted seats.
- **Assessable practice:** Solve by two arguments and reconcile any mismatch by enumerating a small case.

## 4. Represent relationships as graphs

- **Observable capability:** Explain finite relations and demonstrate graph invariants.
- **Teaching blocks:** relation properties; digraphs; connectivity; handshake
- **Original worked example to write:** Represent dependency between fictional club events.
- **Assessable practice:** Check transitivity and parity, and state one property an algorithm book can extend.

## 5. Discrete Probability as a Counting Argument

- **Observable capability:** Construct a finite sample space and use conditioning without confusing it with independence.
- **Teaching blocks:** Sample space, event construction, conditional counts, independence and counterexamples.
- **Original worked example to write:** Compare two anonymous badge draws from a four-item jar, with and without replacement.
- **Assessable practice:** Compute and explain the second-draw probability in both variants; give a paired counterexample to an incorrect independence assumption.
