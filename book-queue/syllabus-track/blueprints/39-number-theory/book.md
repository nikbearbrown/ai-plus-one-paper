# Number Theory (AI Plus One)

**Private Blueprint only. Draft route: `ready-for-draft-review`. Evidence: `matched-source`. No sign-off.**

**Proposed spine:** Build a verified toolkit of arithmetic proofs in Z, transfer it to modular and polynomial settings, and use it to explain what changes in approximation, quadratic rings and analytic number theory.

**Proposed reader:** Two independently adoptable parts: Part I for undergraduates with algebra and introductory discrete proofs; Part II for readers who pass a short congruence, gcd and Euclidean-domain diagnostic, with an abstract-algebra/convergence bridge before advanced work.

**Observed syllabus evidence or limitation:** MATH3527 official Fall 2026 public syllabus pp. 1–2 develops proof and computation over integers, Gaussian integers and polynomial rings, with modular arithmetic, elementary finite-field and quadratic-residue results. Five numbered chapters precede MATH4527's explicitly continuing numbering; no need to copy cryptography sequence or exercises. MATH4527 official Fall 2026 public syllabus pp. 1–2 continues MATH3527's chapter numbering and expects basic number theory, while extending to approximation, Pell equations, quadratic integer rings, ideals/class groups, quadratic forms and Dirichlet series. Chapter 7 elliptic curves explicitly skipped; several listed possibilities were not scheduled.

**Consolidation decision:** Combine MATH 3527 and MATH 4527 in one book. Part I builds arithmetic, congruences and factorization; Part II builds on that foundation. Make the two parts independently adoptable. The separate graduate elliptic-curves course has a distinct prerequisite tier. Merge MATH3527 and MATH4527 as a single Number Theory book with two independently adoptable parts. Shared foundation is divisibility, congruence, units, Euclidean division, and what factorization requires. Split only if independent adoption or length forces separate physical volumes; preserve the shared notation and bridge either way. Keep MATH7359 Elliptic Curves and Modular Forms separate: professional depth in algebraic geometry, finite/complex fields and modular forms is not delivered by the Number Theory sequence.
