# Learning architecture

**Private Blueprint only. Draft route: `ready-for-draft-review`. Evidence: `matched-source`. No sign-off.**

## Proposed measurable outcomes

- Prove integer divisibility and congruence claims with hypotheses and counterexamples.
- Compare the Euclidean algorithm across Z, Z[i], and polynomial rings, identifying conditions for factorization.
- Use quadratic residues and finite-field arithmetic to justify exact small computations.
- Derive continued-fraction/Pell results and contrast element with ideal factorization in quadratic examples.
- Use a reduced binary quadratic form or a convergent Dirichlet series to extend the arithmetic argument and delimit what finite checks prove.

## Sequencing and final artifact

Part I establishes divisibility, modular algorithms and proof, then extends the toolkit to Euclidean domains and quadratic residue problems. Its final verified arithmetic notebook is sufficient for a first course. Part II is independently adoptable after a diagnostic and refresh: approximation leads to quadratic rings and ideal factorization; geometry and series show two new ways to recover arithmetic structure. Its final proof dossier links an explicit example to either a class invariant or an analytic identity. Suggested short bridge: norms, units, gcd, quotient representatives, and convergence. Number theory is one scaffolded book, not a demand that both parts be taught in one term.

## Assessment and AI Plus One practice

Require independent proofs, counterexamples and value predictions before computational or AI-assisted checking. Part I grades exact Euclidean/congruence traces, valid hypotheses and modular proofs; Part II grades valid approximation bounds, comparison of ideal and element arguments, and limits of finite analytic checks. Include low-stakes proof checks and original solutions by part; leave any institution-specific assessment edition for later.

Require a first attempt without AI; document any bounded AI assistance and independently check consequential outputs against evidence. These are original assessment recommendations, not institutional assignments or grading policy.
