# Chapter specifications

**Private Blueprint only. Draft route: `ready-for-draft-review`. Evidence: `matched-source`. No sign-off.**

Each entry is a proposed chapter, not drafted prose.

## 1. Divisibility and the Euclidean algorithm

- **Observable capability:** Find gcds and prove divisibility using the same invariant.
- **Teaching blocks:** Induction; division with remainder; Bézout identity; prime factorization.
- **Original worked example to write:** Use integer combinations of 84 and 30 to establish the least positive attainable value.
- **Assessable practice:** Calculate and prove the gcd for 145 and 89; include a reproducible trace.

## 2. Modular arithmetic as a proof tool

- **Observable capability:** Solve a congruence and reconstruct simultaneous residues.
- **Teaching blocks:** Congruence classes; units; modular inverses; Chinese remainder theorem.
- **Original worked example to write:** Reconstruct an event time seen by three miniature cyclic clocks.
- **Assessable practice:** Explain why one system has no solution and repair a single residue to make it solvable.

## 3. Counting residues and checking modular claims

- **Observable capability:** Use Euler and Fermat to justify finite computations without overgeneralizing.
- **Teaching blocks:** Totient; finite cycles; primality distinctions; counterexample construction.
- **Original worked example to write:** Compare periodicity of powers modulo 15 for a unit and a nonunit.
- **Assessable practice:** Refute a fabricated primality argument with a checked counterexample.

## 4. Arithmetic in new rings

- **Observable capability:** Transfer Euclidean division and identify assumptions for factorization.
- **Teaching blocks:** Integral domains; norms; Euclidean domains; finite fields.
- **Original worked example to write:** Compute a Gaussian-integer gcd and compare with polynomial division over a finite field.
- **Assessable practice:** Provide a complete remainder trace in each ring and explain what does not transfer.

## 5. Squares and a bridge to the advanced part

- **Observable capability:** Determine quadratic solvability and relate ring choice to the proof.
- **Teaching blocks:** Quadratic residues; reciprocity; simple-root lifting; sums of squares.
- **Original worked example to write:** Use congruences and Gaussian arithmetic to explain why 29 is a sum of two squares.
- **Assessable practice:** Prove a solvability claim for two primes and compare with brute-force checks.

## 6. Convergents, error bounds and Pell equations

- **Observable capability:** Use continued fractions to produce and justify integer solutions.
- **Teaching blocks:** Farey neighbors; convergents; error bounds; Pell recurrence.
- **Original worked example to write:** Derive the first nontrivial square-root-of-7 Pell solution and verify directly.
- **Assessable practice:** Compare two rational approximations and prove the guaranteed error bound for a convergent.

## 7. Quadratic integers and broken factorization

- **Observable capability:** Distinguish element and ideal factorization in a concrete order.
- **Teaching blocks:** Quadratic norm; units; irreducibility; ideals.
- **Original worked example to write:** Trace two incompatible irreducible decompositions of 6 in the integers of Q(sqrt(-5)).
- **Assessable practice:** Verify irreducibility by norms and use the example to explain why ideals are introduced.

## 8. Lattices, classes and binary forms

- **Observable capability:** Use a lattice or reduced form to compare quadratic structures.
- **Teaching blocks:** Lattice covolume; Minkowski bound; ideal class; reduction of forms.
- **Original worked example to write:** Enumerate small reduced forms of discriminant -20 and interpret the class-group evidence.
- **Assessable practice:** Provide a candidate reduced form list with a bound and completeness explanation.

## 9. Arithmetic functions and analytic evidence

- **Observable capability:** Derive a Dirichlet-series relationship and state where it holds.
- **Teaching blocks:** Multiplicativity; Euler products; characters; convergence.
- **Original worked example to write:** Compare partial sums and partial prime products for zeta at 2.
- **Assessable practice:** Provide a finite numeric check and independent justification for the infinite identity.
