# Chapter specifications

**Private Blueprint only. Draft route: `hold-source`. Evidence: `tentative-source`. No sign-off.**

Each entry is a proposed chapter, not drafted prose.

## 1. Boundaries before controls

- **Observable capability:** Map legitimate data movement and identify exposed trust edges
- **Teaching blocks:** asset inventory; identity; zones and flows; exposure vs impact
- **Original worked example to write:** A library checkout service has staff, catalog, and guest networks that need clearly differentiated access.
- **Assessable practice:** Draw and defend a least-privilege access matrix for four fictional service relationships.

## 2. What a control buys you

- **Observable capability:** Compare mitigation choices by cost and residual risk
- **Teaching blocks:** segmentation; authentication; patch policy; defense in depth
- **Original worked example to write:** A fictional community center needs guests online but not administrative traffic.
- **Assessable practice:** Evaluate two provided control designs and explain what neither can prevent.

## 3. An alert is not a verdict

- **Observable capability:** Triage a sequence of fixed defensive observations
- **Teaching blocks:** time windows; baseline behavior; sensor blind spots; incident confidence
- **Original worked example to write:** Synthetic access records show unusual outbound volume after a scheduled update.
- **Assessable practice:** Provide two explanations and the safe artifact needed to distinguish them.

## 4. Audit the trust assumption

- **Observable capability:** Check whether a documented control has verifiable coverage
- **Teaching blocks:** least privilege evidence; control ownership; availability; audit notes
- **Original worked example to write:** An isolated community lab has a policy requiring service review but the simulated control inventory omits one service.
- **Assessable practice:** Mark reviewed, missing, and inapplicable controls separately and justify a low-risk remediation priority.

## 5. Contain, recover, learn

- **Observable capability:** Prioritize safe action in a network incident tabletop
- **Teaching blocks:** blast radius; evidence preservation; service continuity; recovery checks
- **Original worked example to write:** A simulated alert affects two classroom devices during a scheduled demonstration.
- **Assessable practice:** Write a containment and rollback decision log scored on protection, evidence, and operational tradeoffs.
