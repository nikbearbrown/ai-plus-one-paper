# Computational Skepticism

**Popular nonfiction · Editorial plan · Not a course or an AI Plus One textbook**

A general reader learns what to do when a machine, an expert, or a persuasive chart sounds certain: identify the claim, inspect the evidence, look for a plausible alternative, and decide what would change their mind. Tell this through recognizable decisions and reported or clearly labeled illustrative examples. Avoid course prerequisites, graded labs, and an expectation that readers install software.

Start by reviewing the [Computational Skepticism for AI manuscript](https://github.com/nikbearbrown/computational-skepticism-for-ai). Choose and check useful examples, fact-check the science, and rewrite for a general audience rather than renaming or repackaging a syllabus. The [Validating AI course blueprint](syllabus-track/blueprints/13-validating-ai/) remains a separate course with objectives, practice, and assessment; the popular book can draw on the same ideas without replacing it. No finished new manuscript or public release is implied.
