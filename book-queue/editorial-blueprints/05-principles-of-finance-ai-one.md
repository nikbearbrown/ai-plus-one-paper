## Reader and purpose

**Audience and prerequisites:** First finance course; percentages, algebra and ability to read simple charts.

**Teaching spine:** Read everyday financing and investing tradeoffs through time value, risk, prices and choice, not software complexity.

## Proposed chapter progression

1. **Money across dates.** Draw cash-flow timelines and compare present values; verify a one-period payment by hand.
2. **Understand securities.** Build a bond and equity claim table; compare what holders receive under two explicitly hypothetical scenarios.
3. **Interpret risk and return.** Make a return distribution and covariance example; distinguish observed variability from promised loss bounds.
4. **Combine assets.** Compare two synthetic portfolios with equal means and different risk; recalculate one weighted return independently.
5. **Connect markets and firms.** Explain how issuing claims funds a project; trace where cash goes without using live prices or giving personal advice.

## Assessed deliverable

Synthetic household-and-firm decision brief: compare one savings schedule, one bond and one diversified alternative. Grade timeline, nominal/real distinction, basic uncertainty and explanation suitable for a novice.

Require a first unaided attempt and a checked revision. Any AI assistance is optional unless the source-verified teaching scope specifically calls for it; assess learner judgment from a separate calculation, trace, or test.
