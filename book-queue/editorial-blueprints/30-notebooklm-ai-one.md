## Reader and purpose

**Audience and prerequisites:** General readers who use digital sources and want a source-centered research workflow, not classroom administration.

**Teaching spine:** Turn a small authorized document collection into questions and inspectable evidence rather than trust a tool summary.

## Proposed chapter progression

1. **Choose and document the collection.** Build a source register with purpose, version, permissions and one intentionally excluded document.
2. **Observe ingestion rather than assume it.** Compare a known statement and missing element before/after import; log discrepancies.
3. **Ask bounded questions with evidence.** Design local evidence and comparison questions; trace each generated clause back to an actual passage.
4. **Test a citation and failure case.** Use a synthetic contradiction and unsupported prompt; record omissions, uncertain context and wrong support.
5. **Make the work reusable and safe.** Create a no-account source-check fallback, redact retained snippets as needed and describe how you would test sharing separately.

## Assessed deliverable

Notebook audit using prepared public or synthetic sources. Grade fit of source set, citation accuracy, unsupported-answer behavior and independent checking of a small numeric claim.

Require a first unaided attempt and a checked revision. Any AI assistance is optional unless the source-verified teaching scope specifically calls for it; assess learner judgment from a separate calculation, trace, or test.
