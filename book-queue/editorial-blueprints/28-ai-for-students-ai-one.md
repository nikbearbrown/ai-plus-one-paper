# AI for Students (AI Plus One)

## Reader and purpose

**Audience and prerequisites:** Secondary or postsecondary learners comfortable with basic academic reading and submitting work.

**Teaching spine:** Use AI only where allowed, maintain a record of the student's own decisions, and prepare to solve a related problem without it.

## Proposed chapter progression

1. **Translate the assignment into a plan.** Write a short personal work plan with allowed and prohibited assistance; flag uncertainty rather than guessing policy.
2. **Keep the first attempt yours.** Draft a claim or solve a toy problem before requesting feedback; compare changes against a clear success criterion.
3. **Query and check sources.** Locate evidence for two claims and show where a plausible generated citation fails.
4. **Revise and disclose honestly.** Produce a change log distinguishing own writing, suggestions considered and rejected material.
5. **Check transfer without the tool.** Use a new example under unaided conditions and compare confidence with actual correctness.

## Assessed deliverable

Produce an original annotated study and writing dossier: project goal, first attempt, bounded tool request, source checks, corrections, disclosure and a novel unaided explanation. Grade evidence and transfer.

Require a first unaided attempt and a checked revision. Any AI assistance is optional unless the source-verified teaching scope specifically calls for it; assess learner judgment from a separate calculation, trace, or test.

## Drafting gate

Verify actual course policy and learners' access locally before examples are used. Keep submissions synthetic and permissions clear; do not design evasion, impersonation or manufactured citations.

Do not treat this compact plan as a manuscript, an approval, or a change to the listed stage.
