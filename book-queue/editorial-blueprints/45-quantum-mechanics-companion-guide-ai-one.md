## Reader and purpose

**Audience and prerequisites:** Students already taking a quantum course who want guided reasoning practice; requires basic state and operator notation.

**Teaching spine:** Use a small set of contrasting problems to recognize what method applies and independently diagnose common quantum mistakes.

## Proposed chapter progression

1. **Choose a representation.** Compare coordinate and finite basis for one guided problem; justify the choice with boundary and cost considerations.
2. **Solve a standard model two ways.** Cross-check a simple spectrum using algebra and a small computation; trace a disagreement to units or settings.
3. **Interpret a measurement sequence.** Annotate probabilities before inspecting worked answers and test how measurement order matters.
4. **Know when to approximate.** Compare a simple approximation with a known exact answer and explain the allowable regime.
5. **Debug a plausible wrong answer.** Use a seeded mistaken derivation to identify logical error, produce a corrected argument and verify a new case.

## Assessed deliverable

An error-correction portfolio: solve an unfamiliar multi-step toy problem, annotate a false solution, and check one numerical benchmark. Grade reasoning and transfer rather than volume of covered theory.

Require a first unaided attempt and a checked revision. Any AI assistance is optional unless the source-verified teaching scope specifically calls for it; assess learner judgment from a separate calculation, trace, or test.
