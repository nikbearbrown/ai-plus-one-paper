## Reader and purpose

**Audience and prerequisites:** Business students or managers with basic understanding of markets and organizations; no law prerequisite.

**Teaching spine:** Make a consequential organizational decision defensible under competing obligations, evidence and stakeholder interests.

## Proposed chapter progression

1. **Separate facts, norms and choices.** Make a claim-and-evidence table for a fictional supplier dispute and identify a factual unknown that changes the decision.
2. **Map responsibility and harms.** List affected groups, control points and a potential distributional impact; validate the map against a counterexample.
3. **Compare ethical frameworks.** Apply two lenses to the same tradeoff and identify where they agree, disagree or omit a concern.
4. **Respond to data and AI dilemmas.** Assess a fictional automated decision with privacy and fairness guardrails; test whether the proposed benefit is actually measured.
5. **Decide, revise and communicate.** Document a decision, dissent and conditions for reconsidering it; evaluate an alternative when new evidence arrives.

## Assessed deliverable

Board-ready ethical decision memo for a fictional operations dilemma. Grade precise harms, defensible comparison using two lenses, dissent and a monitoring or reversal plan; separate ethical reasoning from compliance.

Require a first unaided attempt and a checked revision. Any AI assistance is optional unless the source-verified teaching scope specifically calls for it; assess learner judgment from a separate calculation, trace, or test.
