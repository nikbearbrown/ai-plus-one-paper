## Reader and purpose

**Audience and prerequisites:** General-education readers who can work with fractions and graphs but do not expect to specialize in math.

**Teaching spine:** Choose a sound mathematical representation for social decisions rather than mechanically use a formula.

## Proposed chapter progression

1. **Represent everyday quantities.** Set up fractions, rates and units for meal delivery; check whether totals reconcile after a unit conversion.
2. **Use networks to plan routes.** Draw a graph and compare edge cost with hop count; manually trace two alternatives.
3. **Allocate limited resources.** Compare two toy allocation methods; identify who gains or loses under each rule.
4. **Choose when votes disagree.** Tabulate small ranked ballots and test a rule against an explicit fairness criterion.
5. **Decide with probability and growth.** Contrast expected value and worst case for toy choices; verify a compound-growth check without extrapolating far beyond assumptions.

## Assessed deliverable

Create a reasoned decision poster on allocating rooms at a fictional civic event. Grade transparent assumptions, independent arithmetic, fairness comparison and recognition of a counterexample.

Require a first unaided attempt and a checked revision. Any AI assistance is optional unless the source-verified teaching scope specifically calls for it; assess learner judgment from a separate calculation, trace, or test.
