## Reader and purpose

**Audience and prerequisites:** Educators who can prepare accessible learning materials; managed-account or code-execution features are not prerequisites.

**Teaching spine:** Teach students to trace and critique evidence from a classroom notebook while protecting privacy and preserving equal access.

## Proposed chapter progression

1. **Start with a lesson, not an account.** Define an outcome and two permissioned sources for a deliberate near-disagreement; test whether access is necessary.
2. **Check imports and classroom evidence.** Inspect passages cited in a generated answer; show the difference between genuine contradiction and different populations.
3. **Design age-appropriate independent work.** Write a learner-first task with an accessible no-feature alternative and a novel transfer check.
4. **Verify a teaching calculation.** Compare subgroup rates with a pooled rate using synthetic counts; prove why the unweighted average differs.
5. **Review classroom policy and sharing.** Draft separate teacher/student access checks and scenario debrief; document unavailable features, least-access sharing and a fallback.

## Assessed deliverable

A classroom-ready planning packet using teacher-cleared or synthetic sources: original first attempt, cited comparison, unaided transfer, rubric, privacy/access fallback. Grade specific evidence checks and actual availability versus documented feature claims.

Require a first unaided attempt and a checked revision. Any AI assistance is optional unless the source-verified teaching scope specifically calls for it; assess learner judgment from a separate calculation, trace, or test.
