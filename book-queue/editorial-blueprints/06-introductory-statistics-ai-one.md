## Reader and purpose

**Audience and prerequisites:** Introductory college readers with proportional reasoning and elementary algebra; no calculus.

**Teaching spine:** Trace a statistical conclusion from question to sampling, analysis and defensible interpretation.

## Proposed chapter progression

1. **Ask what the data can represent.** Write population, sample and measurement statements; detect a convenience-sample bias in a toy survey.
2. **Summarize without hiding variation.** Plot distribution and compare median with mean; independently verify a plotted bin and outlier impact.
3. **Use sampling variation.** Simulate repeat samples and compare spread by sample size; check an exact small example.
4. **Estimate and test with uncertainty.** Choose interval or hypothesis test under stated assumptions; distinguish effect size, uncertainty and significance.
5. **Model relationships carefully.** Fit a simple regression; inspect residuals and show why association cannot justify a proposed intervention.

## Assessed deliverable

A short report and reproducible worksheet on simulated bus arrivals. Grade valid denominator, honest uncertainty, fit of method, and a hand-checked statistic; defend an unseen case.
