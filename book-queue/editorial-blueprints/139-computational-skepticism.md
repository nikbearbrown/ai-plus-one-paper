# Computational Skepticism

**Popular nonfiction · Editorial blueprint for author review**

## Reader

General readers who encounter confident tools, persuasive charts, and claims they must act on.

## Thesis and angle

Skepticism is disciplined verification, not default distrust. Follow cases in which the smallest useful check changes a decision.

## Chapter arcs

1. **What exactly was claimed?** Turn a persuasive answer into a proposition that might fail.
2. **The missing denominator:** a chart changes once the reader reconstructs what was counted and omitted.
3. **A second explanation:** keep two plausible stories alive until a check separates them.
4. **The confident machine:** test one generated claim with an independent source or simple counterexample.
5. **When the evidence is still thin:** act, delay, escalate, or reverse course according to stakes and uncertainty.

## Evidence gates

Use verified original evidence for reported cases and label constructed cases explicitly. Record denominator, scope, competing account, and disconfirming evidence. For claims about model reliability give task, version, setting, and test limits.

## Catalog fit

Validating AI (#3) remains a course with practice and assessment. This book has narrative cases and no course prerequisites, graded labs, or required software.
