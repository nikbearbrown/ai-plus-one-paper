## Reader and purpose

**Audience and prerequisites:** First undergraduate quantum course; students know introductory waves, calculus and complex arithmetic.

**Teaching spine:** Learn to derive and test predictions in simple one-dimensional quantum systems without treating a diagram as evidence.

## Proposed chapter progression

1. **From waves to states.** Normalize a simple wavefunction and state which probability a plotted amplitude actually represents.
2. **Quantize a confined particle.** Derive allowed box energies from boundaries and independently check dimensional dependence on length.
3. **Predict changes over time.** Compute relative phases of two stationary components and test how they change a probability density.
4. **Analyze a barrier.** Set up matching conditions and interpret transmission versus reflection; check limiting cases.
5. **Measure and compare predictions.** Compute expectation and variance on a solvable fixture and compare against a numerical simulation with a convergence check.

## Assessed deliverable

Analytic and numerical comparison of a particle in a box and one barrier case. Grade normalization, boundary conditions, probability interpretation and a limiting case checked independently.

Require a first unaided attempt and a checked revision. Any AI assistance is optional unless the source-verified teaching scope specifically calls for it; assess learner judgment from a separate calculation, trace, or test.
