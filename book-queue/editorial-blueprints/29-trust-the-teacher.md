# Trust the Teacher

**Popular nonfiction · Editorial blueprint for author review**

## Reader

Parents, teachers, and readers who want to understand what a classroom asks of a person.

## Thesis and angle

Trust should mean giving teachers room to respond to evidence and students, not suspending scrutiny. Open on one student’s mistaken but revealing answer and trace what a teacher notices, asks, and changes.

## Source basis

[Trust the Teacher outline](https://github.com/nikbearbrown/trust-the-teacher/blob/main/outline.md) is a placeholder, not a narrative plan. The primary chapter inventory already runs from the device ban and the app to teacher discretion, professional tools, cognitive offloading, three measures, equity, and the fix. Preserve and reconcile those drafts first; the arcs below are editorial groupings, not a rewrite or new case record.

## Chapter arcs

1. **A wrong answer worth hearing:** reconstruct the decision the teacher faced before seeing the outcome.
2. **What a teacher can see:** compare a score report with observations of one learner’s reasoning.
3. **When trust needs checking:** follow bias or a mistaken expectation and the evidence that corrects it.
4. **The place of the machine:** compare an automated suggestion with its classroom consequences and show how an adult can overrule it.
5. **Accountable discretion:** show families and schools what to ask for without prescribing every teaching move.

## Evidence gates

Require consent or clearly labeled composites for classroom stories; no identifying student records. Check education claims against primary studies, contexts, and counterevidence. Do not use backup or private Fry material without provenance and permission.

## Catalog fit

A book about teaching and institutional trust, not an assignment redesign workshop or course on AI readiness.
