## Reader and purpose

**Audience and prerequisites:** Business analysts comfortable with tables and regression summaries; causal diagrams introduced from first principles.

**Teaching spine:** Make explicit what action a decision model addresses, which evidence identifies an effect, and what should change when assumptions fail.

## Proposed chapter progression

1. **Start with a decision and intervention.** Write a target population, action, counterfactual and outcome, then contrast two easily confused estimands.
2. **Draw the data-generating assumptions.** Construct a causal diagram and reason about confounding, mediation and a collider.
3. **Choose evidence for an effect.** Compare randomized and observational identification plans with a falsifiable measurement rule.
4. **Stress sensitivity and transfer.** Calculate how a toy unmeasured factor or population shift can reverse a recommendation; separate scenarios from findings.
5. **Keep the model alive after a decision.** Design decision ownership, data refresh, monitoring and revision triggers; identify one situation requiring suspension.

## Assessed deliverable

A decision dossier for a hypothetical training rollout: causal diagram, estimand, identification conditions, sensitivity and a run/update protocol. Grade the distinction between association, intervention and forecast.

Require a first unaided attempt and a checked revision. Any AI assistance is optional unless the source-verified teaching scope specifically calls for it; assess learner judgment from a separate calculation, trace, or test.
