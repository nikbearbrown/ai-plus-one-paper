## Reader and purpose

**Audience and prerequisites:** Business and design students with no legal training. Scope starts with concept recognition and evidence collection, not individual advice.

**Teaching spine:** Identify which kinds of IP questions matter in a fictional project and learn to organize provenance, permissions and referrals.

## Proposed chapter progression

1. **Inventory intangible contributions.** Create a provenance register for source code, design assets and a product name; distinguish authorship from ownership questions.
2. **Compare types of protection.** Classify hypothetical issues across copyright, patents, marks and confidential information; explain at least one ambiguity.
3. **Read permissions instead of guessing.** Build a license checklist and distinguish permission to access from permission to adapt or redistribute.
4. **Analyze collaboration and disclosure.** Trace fictional contribution and publication events; identify facts needing verification before disclosure.
5. **Prepare questions for review.** Write a short, neutral issue memo explaining tradeoffs, unanswered facts and applicable date/jurisdiction boundaries.

## Assessed deliverable

Prepare a rights-and-provenance dossier for a synthetic app with source, creator, proposed uses, documentation and questions for qualified review. Grade distinctions, missing evidence and escalation, not legal conclusions.

Require a first unaided attempt and a checked revision. Any AI assistance is optional unless the source-verified teaching scope specifically calls for it; assess learner judgment from a separate calculation, trace, or test.
